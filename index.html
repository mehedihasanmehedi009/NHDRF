🌿 About Houseplants

Houseplants bring natural beauty into your home. Choosing the right plant depends on sunlight, watering needs, available space, and your lifestyle.

1. Sunlight Requirements

Low-light plants can tolerate less sunlight, while many tropical plants grow best in bright, indirect light. Succulents generally need brighter conditions.

2. Watering Guide

Check the soil before watering. Too much water can damage roots, so use pots with drainage holes.

3. Easy-Care Plants
  • Snake Plant — tolerates low light and infrequent watering.
  • Spider Plant — suitable for many indoor environments.
  • Aloe Vera — prefers bright light and dry soil between watering.
4. Pet Safety

If you have cats or dogs, choose plants known to be non-toxic to pets, such as Spider Plants. Snake Plants and Aloe Vera can be toxic if ingested.

5. General Care Tips
  • Choose a pot with drainage holes.
  • Use suitable soil for your plant.
  • Remove dead or damaged leaves.
  • Check plants regularly for pests.

Popular Houseplants

Snake Plant

A low-maintenance plant suitable for beginners.

About Snake Plant

Snake Plant is a hardy indoor plant with upright, sword-shaped leaves.

  • Light: Low to indirect light
  • Water: Water when soil dries out
  • Care: Beginner-friendly
  • Pet Safety: Toxic to cats and dogs if ingested

Tip: Avoid overwatering and use well-draining soil.

Monstera

A tropical plant with attractive, distinctive leaves.

About Monstera

Monstera is a tropical plant known for its large leaves, which develop distinctive splits as the plant matures.

  • Light: Bright, indirect light
  • Water: Water when the top soil dries
  • Care: Intermediate
  • Pet Safety: Toxic to cats and dogs if ingested

Tip: Give it enough space and use well-draining soil.

Pothos

An easy-care plant with beautiful trailing vines.

About Pothos

Pothos is a popular trailing houseplant that grows well in many indoor environments.

  • Light: Low to bright indirect light
  • Water: Water when the top soil dries
  • Care: Beginner-friendly
  • Pet Safety: Toxic to cats and dogs if ingested

Tip: Trim long vines when needed and avoid soggy soil.
